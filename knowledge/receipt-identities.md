# Receipt identities

Applies when creating, retaining or revising Second impression merchant logos. Implementation and preservation boundaries are in the [production guide](../prototypes/second-impression/production-guide.md).

## Identity

- Give each merchant a specific lettering and composition direction. Do not assign a category icon, font and container independently.
- Make a mark earn its place. Remove ornaments that add no recognisable character or look like stray print defects. A complete wordmark needs no symbol.
- Apply the same scrutiny to retained artwork. Good lettering does not excuse a weak accompanying mark.

## Construction

- Judge the finished silhouette, not the simplicity or complexity of its drawing code. Resolve intended joins into continuous contours; inspect intersections for gaps, tangencies, lumps and uneven counters.
- Relate symbol weight to letter strokes, counters and ink coverage. Do not enlarge a thin pictogram and assume its weight now matches bold lettering.
- Fit lettering through type size, line grouping or redrawing. Never squeeze it horizontally.

## Composition

- Balance the complete lock-up optically. Measure visible glyph and symbol edges, not just text advances or bounding boxes.
- Compare mark-to-name space, line gaps and container insets together. Avoid an isolated symbol with arbitrary padding beside an independently fitted name.
- Check baseline, cap-height and visual-centre relationships. Allow optical corrections rather than imposing identical numerical gaps everywhere.

## Review

- Inspect clean artwork enlarged for contour defects, then at its actual receipt size for weight, legibility and spacing. Print wear cannot rescue unresolved artwork.
- Inspect each identity individually within its unchanged receipt, including nearby subtitles and preambles. Contact-sheet variety is a separate check.
- Reject visible construction or spacing defects before presenting work. Pixel isolation and overflow checks establish preservation, not design quality; visual acceptance belongs to Alex.
