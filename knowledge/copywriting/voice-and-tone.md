# Voice and tone

- Let each prototype's purpose, supplied writing, approved brief and existing copy establish its tone. Use its [README](../product/prototypes.md#readme) for context; keep any agreed tone notes there rather than duplicating prototype descriptions in knowledge.
- Where no tone is established, use plain, direct language. Do not invent a personality or import Alex's portfolio voice into every piece.
- Preserve deliberate humour, bluntness, fragments, unusual cadence and poetic ambiguity when they serve the piece. Do not explain a joke or a poem merely to make it explicit.
- Let expressive text be expressive. Keep instructions, controls, accessible labels and error messages clear enough to act on; humour must not hide the action, failure or recovery step.
- Match the tone to the situation. A playful opening does not require a playful permission request or error message.
- Do not impose first person, a shared catchphrase, compulsory humour or blanket punctuation and word bans across prototypes.
- Read [copy rules](copy-rules.md) alongside local tone choices. Tone may vary; factual accuracy and functional meaning must remain intact.
