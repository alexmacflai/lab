# Library borrowing cards

Forty individually generated circulation-card faces, available in the regular and development paper selectors. The original 20 designs are retained. The approved corner wear and bends use the same material code in both views; receipts remain the initial paper type.

## Reference and design decisions

These are book circulation cards kept in a book pocket and filed during a loan, rather than modern plastic borrower IDs. The [Smithsonian Libraries account of book pockets and date guides](https://library.si.edu/finding-aid/book-pockets-and-date-guides-intricacies-paper-based-library-system) describes the card, pocket, tray and date-stamp system. [Demco's book card specifications](https://www.demco.com/demco-reg-book-cards-author-title-date-loaned-borrower-s-name-date-returned) document 3 × 5 inch medium-weight index Bristol stock, blue print and author/title fields with loan, borrower and return columns. [Demco's catalogue](https://resources.demco.com/ED_Supplies_Perfect_Bound_2019.pdf) shows multiple two-, three- and four-column forms. [Parramatta's historical library collection](https://historyandheritage.cityofparramatta.nsw.gov.au/sites/phh/files/field/media/file/2022-09/Parramatta%20Libraries.pdf) supplies a visual reference for accumulated dates, signatures and worn forms.

The collection uses portrait 76.2 × 127 mm proportions. Cards vary by institutional form, ruling, stock tint, date-stamp colour, typewriter impressions and loan history. The generated names, stamps and accession details are fictional; the book titles identify the variants. None of the source photographs or scans from the references are copied into the prototype.

The faces must feel like functional records: small headings, plausible writing sizes, regular printed tables interrupted by uneven handwritten entries and imperfect rubber date stamps. Blank rows are allowed. The camera print overlays the existing record; there is no cleared photograph box. Avoid uniformly distressed templates, exaggerated yellowing, decorative script, repeated perfect signatures and giant stamps.

## Assets and material

Each `assets/card-NN.webp` comes from a separate image-generation call, not a contact sheet. The full prompt requests a sharp, diffuse flatbed scan of one edge-to-edge card face, with no background, perspective, portrait, silhouette or baked fold shadow. Each call specifies its own form, book record, colours, handwriting and wear. Cards 21–40 extend the same brief with school, county, university, specialist and small lending-library forms. Loan histories are fictional. Dates for the modern titles were corrected to follow their 2016 publication dates ([Hedgerow publisher](https://profilebooks.com/work/a-natural-history-of-the-hedgerow/), [Language of Cities publisher](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/280291/the-language-of-cities-by-sudjic-deyan/9780141980591)). The generated PNG originals remain outside the repository. The faces use the existing 800 × 1333 crop and WebP quality 75, approved after comparing three cards at three compression settings. The 40 runtime images total 3,894,824 bytes, down 61.7% from the quality-90 batch (10,174,624 bytes). Dimensions, designs and material profiles are unchanged. The previous JPEGs remain recoverable from Git; PNG masters remain outside the repository. Generation prompts and source-path mapping are kept in the task's review evidence.

`collection.js` decodes the 40 faces only when selected, using their existing 3:5 crop as 800 × 1333 pixel textures. Native decoded images feed the GPU directly; only worn cards create an extra canvas for edge scuffs. Geometry provides the opaque silhouette, small rounded corners, subtle edge wear, shallow card bow and occasional minor corner bend. Stiff card avoids the receipts' deep folds, torn thermal edges and crumpled-paper motion. Interiors have no colour-key mask or transparent holes. Generated surface texture remains an approximation; review it at close range before promoting it.

This material pass leaves every saved print setting unchanged, including the existing library-card overrides in `dev/settings.js`. The regular library view uses those saved values and restores its original receipt settings when switching back. Ordered dithering, ink transfer, multiply/reflectance compositing, surface highlights and delayed capture use the existing renderer. The portrait, existing record and stock share the same mesh and normals. Per-collection framing fits the shorter card without changing the receipt projection.

## Review and extension

In dev, choose **Library borrowing cards** under **Paper**. The viewer holds the first card; use arrows, thumbnails or **Play proof** to inspect all 40. Turn off **Sample print** to read the raw card, use **Close-up** for fibres and marks, and choose **Surface normals** or **Silhouette** for geometry checks. Live camera options and the print controls apply to either collection.

The desktop viewer stays on the left while grouped controls sit on the right. On narrow screens they stack. Collection thumbnails and optional comparisons sit below. Print-condition and surface-comparison renderers initialise only when opened. Switching collections cancels old thumbnail work and releases their GPU resources and event handlers; it keeps the camera stream, print settings and movement settings.

For future additions: inspect every face for malformed text, repeated handwriting, implausible wear, baked lighting, edge artefacts and portrait clipping. Check opaque interiors, card proportions, live switching in both directions, camera pause/start, drawer keyboard focus, phone layout and complete playback cycles. This is a fictional collection rather than a historically accurate archive. Add paper types through `paper-types.js`, with a label, count, lazy loader and public-release flag. Never load card assets during receipt-only startup.

## Subtle material comparison

In dev, **View → Card material** switches between **Approved** (the default, also used in the regular view) and **Original**, without changing print controls or the selected card. Across 40 cards, 24 keep their original surface, ten have light corner wear and six have a local bend. The original 20 retain their approved profiles: corner wear on 3, 7, 11, 15 and 19; a lifted lower corner on 5; edge bends on 12 and 18. Profiles stay fixed to each card, with no repeating waves or receipt-style crumpling. Additional lift is at most 2.7 mm on 76.2 mm-wide stock.

**Surface comparisons** shows matched original/approved pairs for the changed cards and one unchanged control. The image, impression, settings, lighting and placement match within each pair. Choose **Portrait**, **Grid and solid ink**, **Paper only** or **Surface normals**. Face, writing and camera print share the mesh and contact-map normals. No print shader, highlight gain, halftone or transfer texture changes are part of this pass.

## Compression review

[Compression review](../dev/compression/) contains nine isolated candidates: three strategies on cards 1, 7 and 12. A uses WebP quality 75 at 800 × 1333; B uses WebP quality 90 at 640 × 1067; C uses JPEG quality 80 at 800 × 1333. Each is encoded from the same quality-90 reference asset, with stable baseline copies. The page offers fitted cards, matched 100% detail crops, synchronised full-image scrolling and exact file-size comparisons. Option A was approved and has been applied to all 40 runtime images. The comparison files remain a record of the decision; collection loaders use only the runtime assets.
