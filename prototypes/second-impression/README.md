# Second impression

A collection of 100 authored receipts across 24 design families, including the original six reference receipts. Headers use original pictograms, seals, reverse-print panels and varied wordmarks. Paper conditions range from pristine to pocket-crumpled, with damage fixed per ticket. Twenty-four receipts have one, two or three neatly spaced full-width folds, with mirrored small tilts on successive creases. Thirty-three tickets carry a stain, stroke, note or doodle, including seven coffee stains roughly as wide as the receipt. Notes and eight different doodles use generated scanned-style textures rather than fonts or geometric icons. Each entry has its own transaction, layout settings, stock and edges. Dry-transfer coverage samples a generated material in paper coordinates, combined with contact from the receipt mesh. The camera print keeps its coarse ordered dithering, with restrained finer variation inside the ink deposits. Each frame independently varies its neutral-black ink: most prints are clean or lightly textured, with occasional weak transfer, stronger bleed or fold-related marks. It multiplies into the receipt before the shared surface is lit and rendered, so paper and print bend together. Tight crease compression and restrained highlights over the ink reveal the folds. A geometry-derived shadow follows lifted edges. The receipts use authored layouts and a generated paper material rather than real scans. Camera frames remain in memory on your device and are never uploaded or saved.

## How to use

- Open the main page on localhost or HTTPS. Each visit starts on a random receipt; playback continues in order through all 100 and wraps around. The receipt fits the space above the camera bar, up to approximately 800 CSS pixels tall. A centred spinner appears while the first receipt loads; the receipt and controls then appear together. Your supplied photograph appears through the print filter before camera use.
- Print conditions and their texture placement vary with each new frame. Fold-related effects follow the same mesh normals as the paper. Stopping the camera holds the final impression.
- Select **Start camera** and allow access. The filled button becomes a pale button without a visible border while starting or running, then returns to filled when stopped. The current receipt stays still until the first delayed camera print is ready, then cycles at up to 12 fps. Playback uses a 12 fps schedule and advances one receipt per frame. The next paper is prepared during idle time. **Stop camera** releases the camera and holds the last receipt and print. **Cancel** abandons a pending camera request.
- On phones and short windows, open **Camera options** using the icon beside the camera button. Wider tablet and desktop layouts show both toggles beside a smaller camera button. **Keep me in frame** starts on; **Remove background** starts off. Both use toggles. The opaque drawer slides up from below the screen without shrinking the receipt; closing it also animates. Close it with its close button, Escape or by tapping outside it.
- In compact layouts, **Back camera** switches to the rear-facing camera. Front-camera prints are mirrored; back-camera prints retain their natural orientation. Switching releases the old stream and waits for a new delayed print. If the back camera is unavailable, the prototype falls back to the front camera.
- Person detection downloads when you first start the camera with either option enabled. Camera images stay on your device. The options target people and may miss hair, hands or held items. A detection failure turns both options off and shows a message; the full camera frame remains available.
- Native Apple **Center Stage**, where supported, remains in the system camera menu. Avoid combining it with digital framing if the result moves too much.
- If camera access is denied, allow it in your browser's site settings and try again. Loading and graphics failures show a refresh message. The page needs WebGL 2 and has no build step.

## Development view

Open [the development page](dev/) for a sticky viewer, grouped controls, collection selection and the eight-condition print comparison. It shares the public page's `second-impression-player.js`, renderer, camera processing and receipt assets; both pages use one collection.

- Select a thumbnail, use the arrow buttons or press Left/Right to hold a receipt. **Previous 20** and **Next 20** browse all five gallery pages.
- **Play proof** cycles receipts without the camera; **Pause proof** holds one. Proof playback cannot skip the initial camera-buffer wait.
- **Sample print** shows the filtered photograph. Disable it to inspect paper alone. Development camera options start off so an unprocessed camera feed is available for comparison.
- Use **Close-up**, **Background** and **Surface** to inspect folds, silhouettes, normals and edge defects.
- Under **Edit values**, choose **Global** or **Paper type**. Editing paper-type values keeps them separate from the shared defaults. Each property has an override checkbox in **Paper type** mode. Its label appears as a tooltip and is available to screen readers. Check it to apply that property’s specific value to every paper in the selected type (receipts or library borrowing cards). Uncheck it to use the global value while preserving the specific value for later. Image and timing properties can each inherit or override independently. Overrides are keyed by paper type and stay the same while stepping through its individual papers.
- **Resolution** controls the halftone grid width, from 48 to 288 pixels; its height follows **Aspect ratio**. Ratios include 4:5, 2:3 and 3:4 portrait, square, and 4:3, 3:2 and 16:9 landscape. The source portrait is centre-cropped to fit. **Print width** ranges from 25% to 200% of paper width, with oversized prints clipped by the paper silhouette. **Image gamma** retains its existing response. **Mid-light brightness** lifts midtones and highlights towards white while protecting the deepest shadows. These image controls update the main preview and print comparisons; **Current print** retains the earlier fixed image treatment.
- To save directly into the project, run `python3 tools/second-impression-dev-server.py` from the repository root and open <http://127.0.0.1:8082/prototypes/second-impression/dev/>. **Save to project** writes global values and property overrides to `dev/settings.js`, which the development page loads on the next visit. This changes the local source file; it does not commit or publish it. **Copy values** copies the same JavaScript module to the clipboard. Other static servers can preview and copy settings but cannot save to the project. Camera images are never included in settings.
- **Hold heavy bleed** stops the camera and receipt, selects rare heavy bleed and enables close-up. Press it again for a fresh impression. The frame label shows its seed alongside the condition, so you can identify the example when sharing feedback.
- **Eight print conditions** compares clean printing, ordinary printing, weak transfer, moderate bleed, local heavy bleed, weak transfer with bleed, fold-related transfer and ridge smear. Cards use one receipt and the same photograph. Select a card or **Main print** to try it in the main preview, including with the camera. **Current print** restores the earlier fixed treatment for comparison.
- **Detail crops** changes the comparison cards only. **Print view** shows ink coverage, bleed radius, print-time contact or the transfer mask itself. Cards render at the screen’s pixel density, up to 3×. **Random each frame** independently chooses a condition for each new animation frame, with clean prints most common and heavy bleed rare. Texture position, orientation, grain and strength also vary. The coverage material uses a fixed 0.5× physical scale. **Randomise prints** previews fresh impressions on one held receipt at the selected frame rate; a named condition keeps that condition while randomising its details. Pause to hold one impression. Camera use stops this held-receipt preview; new camera frames still receive fresh impressions. Receipt thumbnails retain the existing print treatment.
- Open **Timing and movement**, or press **D**, for frame rate, delay and separate **Paper hand placement** and **Print hand placement** sliders, plus live condition, fold, mark and buffer values. Unsaved changes reset on reload. Use **Edit values → Paper type** and the checkbox beside each placement slider to override it independently for all papers in the selected type. A zero value holds that layer steady during playback. Existing hand-placement values were copied to both sliders, preserving the starting motion. Saved placement overrides also apply in the public view for available paper types.

See the [production guide](production-guide.md) for material provenance, physical dimensions, rendering limits and verification criteria.

The expandable **Surface comparisons** section starts with Small Hours before and after the edge fix, holding folds, lighting and the ink impression constant. Cut-edge deformation is confined to the perimeter in the public and dev renderers. Further pairs compare **Previous crease** and **Sharper crease** on Noodle House, tidy, folded and crumpled surfaces, keeping the height and highlight shader the same. Switch to **Grid and solid ink** to inspect bending and crease highlights, or **Surface normals** to inspect geometry. The sharper crease mapping and highlights are also used in the public view. The comparison includes all three neat-fold patterns.

## Library borrowing cards

The regular and development views include **40 library borrowing cards** under **Paper**. Each has an individually generated face based on book circulation forms, with author/title fields, ruled loan records, date stamps and handwriting. Their 3 × 5 inch proportions, rounded corners and restrained stiff-card bends differ from receipts. The original 20 designs are retained. Card assets load only when selected; receipts remain the initial paper type.

Both views use the approved materials: mostly preserved cards, occasional corner wear and gentle bends. In dev, **View → Card material** switches between **Approved** and **Original**. **Surface comparisons** holds the ink and placement constant across matched pairs, including an unchanged control. The regular library view uses the saved card-specific print values; switching back restores its original receipt settings. No print values or ink effects are changed by this expansion.

The collection thumbnails show unprinted records. Turn off **Sample print** to inspect the full face. On desktop, the viewer stays to the left of grouped controls; on smaller screens they stack. Collection thumbnails and expandable comparisons sit below. Changing paper keeps the camera stream and releases old collection renderers. See the [library card production guide](library-cards/production-guide.md) for research and provenance.

All 40 library images use the approved **WebP quality 75** at the existing **800 × 1333** resolution. The batch is 3.89 MB, down from 10.17 MB at quality 90 (61.7% smaller). [Compression review](dev/compression/) retains the three-card comparison and file sizes that informed this choice. Designs, print values and material profiles are unchanged by compression.

## Torn notebook scraps

The public and development Paper selectors include 40 torn notebook scraps across five families of eight: ruled, squared, plain, narrow ruled and graph paper. Surviving widths are 64–76 mm; height-to-width ratios stay within 1.15–1.30. The original six pieces retain their stock and fold profiles. The additional 34 combine individual tear seeds, stock tints, ruling offsets, handwritten notes and factory-edge selections. Two generated 4 × 5 handwriting atlases supply short notes; a generated 2 × 4 atlas supplies four handwritten passages and four doodle clusters. Eight composition patterns place these at different angles, combine multiple marks or crop a larger passage at the torn boundary. Labelled doodle clusters retain the scale of a 100–112 mm notebook area and are cropped by the surviving scrap, rather than shrinking a whole blueprint or map into a corner. The annotation atlas is simulated handwriting on white, without paper edges, ruling or baked-in folds. These are simulated paper objects, not scans. Gallery pages show 20 at a time. Most pieces are nearly flat or neatly folded; crumpled papers remain deferred.

For the highlight comparison, select **Page corner · quarter fold**, then open **Print conditions**. All eight cards use that same paper, photograph and print settings. Turn off **Detail crops** to compare full scraps. The shared surface lighting applies after every print condition; diagnostics such as **Ink coverage** intentionally omit lighting. The saved print settings are unchanged by this proof.

The [torn-edge proof](dev/notebook-edge-proof.html) compares the committed fibrous edges and proposed material-based right/bottom edges at normal size and in enlarged crops. Its Paper selector previews a nearly flat page corner, a half-folded side-edge piece and a quarter-folded piece torn on all four sides. Each comparison preserves that paper’s factory edges and folds. The proposed proof uses broader, asymmetric hand-tear trajectories derived from the supplied references; the committed comparison retains the earlier straighter outlines. The proposed edge refines the committed fibre treatment with non-repeating fracture widths, opaque exposed pulp, clustered fine strands and small torn tongues. Clear gaps separate the fibre tips; the receiving paper has no gradient border or generated edge-image overlay. Its partial-alpha fringe stays inside the existing mesh envelope. `notebook-edge-baseline.js` retains the committed fibrous treatment for comparison. The proposed notebook treatment shortens loose fibres, reduces bundle density and narrows the exposed pulp band while retaining the visible tear scale. Outline geometry, folds and saved print settings are unchanged. All 40 scraps in the main dev collection use the refined hand-tear outlines and thin torn-edge treatment. Each retains its existing factory edges and fold profile. This is a rendered approximation of a tear, not a physical fracture simulation.

Notebook collection loading prepares the torn-edge strips once. A 96 MiB CPU cache retains those narrow strips; playback rebuilds the paper at the existing texture resolution and keeps the shared eight-paper GPU cache. This moves the fibre drawing out of the camera loop without changing folds or print settings.

Paper references supplied on 20 November 2026 are retained in [dev/paper-references/](dev/paper-references/). Reference 01 governs larger tear-path wandering; 02 shows exposed layers on thicker stock, scaled down for notebook paper; 03–05 show torn outlines, fine fibres and retained factory edges. References 06–09 are reserved for later crumpled and neatly folded material work, including the grid-paper example. They are visual references, not textures pasted onto the proof. The current pass keeps the existing folds.
